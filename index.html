Shelf Number

A shelf number (also call number or shelf mark) is a unique code used in libraries, acting like an address to locate a specific book.

The Tamizdat Project Archive “From 2014 to the Present” documents present-day publications by authors who have been forced into exile or can no longer publish in their own country. Shelf Number, a special project, traces the journey of these émigré and tamizdat editions, mapping the library shelves that now hold them and exploring the presence of Tamizdat editions across the globe.

          About the Project

          Shelf Number aims to map the libraries that hold editions of the fifth wave of tamizdat, to explore the presence and availability of these editions, and the shape of the collections that hold them.

          Source

          1. Tamizdat Project Archive (2014–present)
          2. WorldCat

          Considerations

          • Incomplete data. The collection remains incomplete, as new material is published almost every day. Some publishers are underrepresented owing to the lack of information available online, and library catalogue updates also take time. Not all libraries are registered within the WorldCat system either, so their holdings may not appear here.
          • Historical perspective matters. Any attempt to analyse processes in real time is nearly impossible, given the number of unknown variables and uncertainties.
          • Inconsistencies. There may be inconsistencies in the data: while publishers and libraries share standardized data formats, some records are still inconsistent and may affect the statistics.

          Our goal is not to provide definitive conclusions, but rather to step back, take a broader view, and explore this wave.

          Shelf Number is an ongoing project, and the data will be updated as it moves forward.
